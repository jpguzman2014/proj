# Licensing and territory decisions

Why the app is built the way it is, so the reasoning survives the people who made
it. Not legal advice. A lawyer in the operating jurisdiction signs the final
wording and reviews any deal named here.

## The one decision everything else follows from

**No publisher media is stored, served or hot-linked. Ever.**

Game screenshots, cover art, character renders, logos and wordmarks are owned by
publishers. Finding one online does not make it redistributable, and an API that
serves them does not grant a copyright licence. An aggregator's terms are a
contract between you and the aggregator. They do not bind Nintendo.

Fair use will not carry a paid product across the markets in scope. In the US it
is a fact-specific defence you only win after being sued. UK fair dealing is a
closed list of purposes and a quiz is not among them. EU exceptions are a closed
list too. Obligations follow the players, so the weakest jurisdiction sets the
position.

Enforcement in practice is a takedown notice and removal from an app store. Both
cost the publisher almost nothing and cost the product its launch.

So clue art is generated by `src/media/generatedArt.ts` from each entry's own
metadata, and app icons by `scripts/make-icons.mjs`. Those bytes originate here.

### If licensed media is ever added

`MediaProvider` in `src/media/provider.ts` is the seam. It already carries
per-asset `credit` and `license`, and the UI already renders them, so credit
cannot be dropped by accident. Adding a source means registering a provider, not
editing the game.

Two candidates and where they actually stand:

| Source | Commercial use | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| IGDB | Not on the free tier. Needs a partnership agreement. | Contact partner@igdb.com |
| RAWG | Free below their caps | Attribution plus a live hyperlink on every page using the data. No redistribution. |

RAWG's free ceiling is 100,000 monthly active users, 500,000 page views per
month, and 20,000 API requests per month.

Neither agreement resolves publisher copyright in the underlying images. Both
reduce contract risk with the aggregator only. Read that sentence twice before
budgeting for either.

## Metadata

Facts are not copyrightable. Release years, developer names and platform lists
are facts, and *Feist* settled that in the US.

The trap is elsewhere. The EU has a separate database right protecting
substantial extraction from a compiled database even when every individual fact
is free. The risk is bulk-copying one commercial catalogue, not using the facts.

Current position: `src/data/` is compiled by hand. That is clean, and it is small.

Planned position: import from **Wikidata**, released under CC0, a public domain
dedication that permits bulk commercial copying with no attribution owed. The
`sources` field on `Game` exists for the importer to fill so every row can name
where it came from.

## Trademarks

Naming a game or a character in a quiz is nominative use. It is protected in the
US under the *New Kids on the Block* three-part test, and mirrored by Article 14
of the EU trade mark regulation and section 11 of the UK Trade Marks Act 1994.
This is why the quiz itself is not the exposure.

Logos and wordmarks are a different question, because they use the mark's
distinctive form rather than just the name. None appear in the app, and none
should.

A commissioned drawing of a copyrighted character is a derivative work and
infringes. "We drew it ourselves" does not rescue a drawing of Kratos.

## User-generated content

There is none, deliberately.

Party display names are picked from `shared/nicknames.json`, never typed, and the
server replaces anything outside that vocabulary. Answers are multiple choice.
Data reports are free text but never leave the device.

That removes the app from user-to-user online safety regimes, including the UK
Online Safety Act, whose illegal-harms duties became enforceable in March 2025
and whose children's risk assessment fell due in July 2025. Small services are
not exempt from those duties, so staying out of scope is worth far more than a
free-text field.

**This holds only while all three remain true.** Adding typed names, chat,
reactions, or server-side syncing of report notes puts the product back in scope
and triggers a risk assessment, a reporting route and moderation duties.

## Territories

Withholding is enforced by the payment provider and the app stores, which
maintain the sanctions lists and geo-block on your behalf. Do not hand-maintain
this list. Configure it in their dashboards, then mirror it onto the refunds and
availability page so the page and the enforcement never disagree.

**Excluded because of sanctions.** Selling here can be a criminal matter rather
than a civil one, under US OFAC and the EU and UK regimes:

- Russia
- Belarus
- Iran
- North Korea
- Syria
- Cuba
- The Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk regions of Ukraine

**Excluded because of market entry cost, not risk:**

- China. Requires an ICP filing and, for a game, a publication licence from the
  NPPA. Not achievable without a local publisher.

**Confirm against your provider's own list before launch.** Sanctions move, and
the provider's list is the one that actually gates the transaction.

## Age ratings

A product sold as a game needs ratings in several markets. Germany, Brazil,
Australia and South Korea all have their own boards.

The practical route is the International Age Rating Coalition questionnaire. One
submission produces ratings for Google Play, the Microsoft Store and Nintendo,
covering ESRB, PEGI, USK, ClassInd, ACB and GRAC. Apple does not use it and has
its own age rating system, filled in during App Store submission.

Content here is a quiz about games, so the expected outcome is the lowest or near
lowest band in every regime. Submit anyway. An unrated game is a removal reason.

## Tax

Selling digital services worldwide triggers VAT or GST registration in a great
many jurisdictions, each with its own threshold and filing calendar.

Sell through app stores or a merchant of record such as Paddle or Lemon Squeezy.
They become the seller of record and handle registration, collection and filing.
Taking card payments directly means owning that problem in every market you sell
into, which is not a reasonable burden for a quiz app.
