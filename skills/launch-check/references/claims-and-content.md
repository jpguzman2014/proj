# Honest Content

## Fake reviews

Illegal, specifically and by name, under the US FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and
Testimonials effective 2024, the EU Omnibus Directive, and the UK Digital Markets,
Competition and Consumers Act 2024. The FTC rule carries civil penalties per violation.

Remove: testimonials for customers who do not exist, stock photos presented as real
customers, AI-generated review text, template placeholder reviews, review counts or star
ratings not derived from real reviews, and undisclosed incentivised or employee reviews.

Template placeholders are the usual culprit in a generated site. "Amazing service! -
Sarah J." shipped from a theme is a fake review, regardless of intent.

If real reviews exist, publish them accurately: do not suppress negative ones
selectively, disclose any incentive, and do not edit the substance.

## Unsupported claims

Advertising law across major jurisdictions requires substantiation held **before** the
claim is published. Claims needing evidence on file include superlatives such as best,
#1, leading, or fastest; any statistic or percentage; guarantees; comparisons to named
competitors; certifications and awards; environmental claims, now specifically targeted
by EU and UK greenwashing rules; and health, medical, or financial outcome claims, which
carry the heaviest penalties.

Test each claim: is there a document on file that proves it? If not, cut it or reduce it
to something true. "Trusted by thousands" with 40 customers is a false statement.

Beware the generated-copy default. Filler marketing text arrives full of confident
superlatives that no one ever checked.

## Image and font copyright

Every image, icon, illustration, and font needs a traceable licence. Not cleared by
default: anything from an image search; anything from Pinterest or a social platform;
competitor screenshots; brand logos, which need trademark permission; recognisable
people, who need a model release; and recognisable private property, which may need a
property release.

AI-generated images carry their own exposure. Output resembling a real brand, a
trademarked character, or an identifiable person is a live risk regardless of the
generator's terms.

Keep a `CREDITS.md` or an asset manifest recording the source, licence, and any required
attribution for each asset. Honour attribution requirements; a Creative Commons BY image
without the credit is an infringing use.

Fonts have licences too. Desktop licences frequently do not cover webfont embedding.

## Local laws

Obligations follow where the customers are, not where the server is. Establish the
jurisdictions in scope, then note the additions each brings.

| Region | Adds |
|---|---|
| EU / EEA | GDPR, ePrivacy, Omnibus, 14-day withdrawal, accessibility act |
| UK | UK GDPR, PECR, DMCC Act, Equality Act 2010 |
| California | CCPA/CPRA, a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link |
| Other US states | A growing set of comprehensive privacy statutes |
| Canada | PIPEDA, CASL, which is strict on email consent |
| Australia | Privacy Act, Australian Consumer Law |
| Brazil | LGPD |

Selling to consumers usually means their local consumer law applies whatever the terms
say about governing law. Some categories, among them health, finance, alcohol, gambling,
and anything aimed at children, add licensing regimes well beyond this checklist.

Regulated categories and multi-jurisdiction sales are the point at which to buy an hour
of a lawyer's time rather than rely on a checklist.
